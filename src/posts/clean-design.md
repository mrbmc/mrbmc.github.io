# Clean Design

Please, please, do not call a design "clean". It's deeply problematic for design critique, and carries some heavy cultural baggage.

There are a few reinforcing problems with "Clean" as a descriptor:

1. It's imprecise and ambiguous. This is lazy critique that doesn't precisely name what is actually happening.
2. It's a euphemism for minimalism / whiteness / emptiness, without naming the class baggage that comes with modernism.
3. It carries hsitorical weight by equating cleanliess as purity, as control, as sterilization. It's a proxy for moral goodness.

These three problems reinforce each other. The imprecision makes the baggage invisible. The baggage makes the imprecision feel morally neutral.

The first big problem with clean is it doesn't actually describe anything specific in design terms. Are you saying the design is defect free? Most digital designs are since they're rendered by machine. Are you saying the design is using a color palette that is light or low contrast? Are you describing the layout and commenting on the use of negative space (a true form of minimalism)? Are you saying the style is minimalist instead of maximalist, and free of adornment (or some would say personality)? 

"Clean" could mean one or all of those things, therefore it means nothing.

Let's assume for a second that "clean" is actually a proxy for minimal. The type of bauhuas minimalism popularized by Apple, Ikea, or Tesla. If minimalism is what we think "good" design is, then we have to also grappl with the fact that minimalism is laden with class issues. Minimalism is not inherently racist but it is classist which is basically the same thing. The problem is minimalism is not accessible to most people. It requires a level of income that can buy its way out of advertising supported media, mass manufactured products, and the space constraints that hide the clutter of every day living, and the education to navigate those constraints. Anything that isn't attainable by most people is inherently elitist, and a product of the systemic racism that separates elite sensibilities from populist realities.



Using "clean" as a descriptor for design is a little racist. Maybe a lot racist.
