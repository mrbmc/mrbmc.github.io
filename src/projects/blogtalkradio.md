## A profitable platform with nowhere to grow

BlogTalkRadio was profitable in 2013 and stuck. Popular hosts (10k+ listens per episode) were getting poached by larger platforms. The rest of the catalog, millions of listens spread across mid-audience shows, couldn't be sold to advertisers at scale. Subscription revenue had flattened.

I joined as the first product and design hire. The strategic policy was a pivot from SaaS to ad-supported, with a revenue share back to hosts. I co-authored it with the cofounder/COO and the GM/VP of product. The pivot only worked if three things came together in a specific order: prove the ad technology, build revenue against it, then invest in the host experience that the rev share was supposed to retain.

Sequencing that order, aligning the team around it, and shipping it phase by phase was my specific contribution. Doing it in any other order broke the model.

## 1. Prove the technology

Podcast ad campaigns have a four-week shelf life. Most BTR listens came weeks, months, or years after recording. Premium CPMs ($30 to $100) required 10k listens per campaign, and burn-in ads expired before listens accumulated. The math didn't work.

{% include "images/portfolio/btr/btr-listens-decay-curve.svg" %}  

The call was to insert ads at download time, not at recording time. Every play encodes the episode with fresh creative. Real-time ad insertion for MP3s did not exist in 2013. We built it. The architecture became patent US20170195373A1.

Until that worked, nothing else was worth designing.

## 2. Build revenue against it

Patented tech is not a business. The next phase was productizing the inventory. We bundled episodes, hosts, and categories into media products that cleared the 10k threshold for top-tier CPMs. Ad revenue grew 100% in the first year.

This revenue was the precondition for phase 3. Creator tools and a rev share don't exist without it.

## 3. Invest back into hosts

Only once revenue was real did the user-facing investment make sense. The creator tools shipped when I joined offered technical guidance on sound and distribution. Nothing on the work of making a show people want to listen to.

We mapped the host journey to align product, design, engineering, marketing, and community management on the same leverage points.

!['Journey Map'](/images/portfolio/btr/btr-customer-experience-map-hosts.png){.full-width}
Starting A Show{.caption #journeymap}

From that map we built features into the authoring tools. **Signal Strength** scored title, description, and intro completeness. **Template Episodes** scaffolded repeatable show structures. Analytics showed the first thirty seconds of an episode determined whether a listener kept going, so the tools focused there.

The dashboard surfaced what hosts cared about most: listen counts. We contextualized those metrics and paired them with the content guidance, so the numbers came with a path for moving them.

Cover art was where the host and business interests aligned cleanly. Hosts were subject matter experts, not designers, and their artwork was costing them audience. I directed an outside team of graphic designers to overhaul the top 200 shows, set creative direction, and ran review. Hosts grew their audiences. Advertiser CPMs rose against a better-presented catalog. One intervention served both sides of the rev share.

![New Artwork](/images/portfolio/btr/btr-host-artwork.png){.full-width .addLightbox}

For the live broadcast, we rebuilt the studio around what UX research and ride-alongs surfaced: hosts need omnipresent state feedback, pre-recorded intros and outros should be treated as content, hosts can't tell which callers want to speak on air, and pre-show promotion is part of the workflow. The art direction borrowed from professional audio and video authoring tools.

## The dynamo turned

Each phase fed the next. Better ad inventory funded creator tools. Better tools improved content quality. Better content drove engagement up 200% YoY. Higher engagement raised CPMs against the inventory we'd built.

{% include "images/portfolio/btr/btr-dynamo-illustration.svg" %}

BlogTalkRadio was acquired by iHeartMedia for $50M. Ad revenue grew 37% YoY. Subscription revenue grew 42% YoY. Host activation tripled. The ad insertion architecture was granted US patent US20170195373A1.

The design case at BTR was never about screens. It was about whether design and product could sequence a structural change in how the company made money.
